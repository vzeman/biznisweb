## Editorial boundaries

- Tensile and tear strength must be distinguished from seam strength, abrasion resistance, snagging, and bursting strength.
- Drape must be explained as a whole-fabric response influenced by bending stiffness, mass, construction, direction, finish, moisture, and garment cut. It must not be reduced to fibre name alone.
- Thermal resistance must be separated from water-vapour resistance, wind protection, subjective warmth, and complete-garment performance.
- UPF must be explained as a tested fabric or garment property, not inferred from colour or opacity alone. The article must state that clothing protects only covered areas and does not replace shade, a hat, sunglasses, or sunscreen on exposed skin.
- Home care advice must start with the garment label. No universal wash temperature or unsupported restoration claim.
- Articles use current-date publication, clean explicit slugs, no fixed prices, and no public workflow terminology.

## Technical basis

- ISO 13934-1:2013, confirmed in 2024, for maximum force and elongation by strip method.
- ISO 13937-2:2000, confirmed in 2023, for single-tear force with trouser-shaped specimens.
- ISO 9073-9:2008 for drapability and drape coefficient; ASTM D1388-23 for bending length and flexural rigidity.
- ISO 11092:2026 for thermal and water-vapour resistance under steady-state sweating-hotplate conditions; ASTM F1868-23 for the limits of translating material results to worn clothing.
- AATCC TM183 for UV transmission and UPF measurement; WHO and ARPANSA for consumer protection boundaries.
