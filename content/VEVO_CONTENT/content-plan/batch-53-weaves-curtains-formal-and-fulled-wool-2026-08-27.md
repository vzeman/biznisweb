# VEVO batch 53: weave, curtain, formal textile and fulled wool

## Purpose

This batch deliberately covers four different consumer decisions: identifying a broken-twill pattern, washing a lightweight curtain, handling a crisp formal fabric and distinguishing three related wool constructions. The articles must answer the everyday question first and then explain the fibre, construction, finishing and whole-product limits behind the recommendation.

## Articles

1. `co-je-rybia-kost-lamany-keper-smer-vzoru-a-spravne-pranie`
   - Explain herringbone as an offset broken-twill effect and keep chevron as a separate pointed construction.
   - Cover fibre-neutral identification, orientation, matching at seams, skew, fraying, washing and low-pressure pressing.
2. `co-je-voalova-zaclona-priesvitna-tkanina-prach-a-pranie-bez-pokrčenia`
   - Focus on voál as a curtain system with hooks, heading tape, weights, seams and accumulated airborne dust.
   - Cover dry preparation, load size, snag prevention, whitening boundaries, damp hanging and cases where ironing remains necessary.
3. `co-je-taft-sustava-tkanina-vodne-mapy-a-bezpecne-cistenie`
   - Explain crisp plain weave, rib effect, lustre and finish without treating taft as a synonym for silk.
   - Cover water rings, perspiration, permanent creases, seam impressions, structured dresses, linings and professional-cleaning limits.
4. `co-je-loden-a-varena-vlna-valchovanie-plst-a-bezpecne-cistenie`
   - Keep loden, boiled wool and felt in one terminology article because they share controlled fibre entanglement but differ in base construction.
   - Cover reversible soil versus irreversible felting, dry brushing, spot tests, reshaping, flat or supported drying, storage and moth inspection.
