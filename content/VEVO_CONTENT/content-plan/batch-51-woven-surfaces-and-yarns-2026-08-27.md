## Purpose

This batch extends the material encyclopaedia with four distinct questions that shoppers meet in shirts, dresses, bedcovers, blankets and upholstered furniture. The articles explain not only what the trade name means, but which part of the finished product controls washing, stain treatment, drying and repair.

## Articles

1. `co-je-chambray-farebna-osnova-svetly-utok-a-spravne-pranie`
   - Explain the usual plain-weave, yarn-dyed colour effect and boundary from denim, poplin and Oxford cloth.
   - Cover collar and cuff soil, colour transfer, ironing, shrinkage and garment construction.
2. `co-je-matelasse-plasticky-tkany-relief-zrazanie-a-starostlivost`
   - Explain how a woven or compound construction can imitate quilting without a separately inserted padded layer.
   - Cover relief flattening, differential shrinkage, bedcover care, pressing and the boundary from true quilted products.
3. `co-je-zenilka-vlasova-priadza-uvolnovanie-chlpkov-a-cistenie`
   - Explain pile held by core yarns and distinguish chenille from bouclé, velvet and ordinary plush.
   - Cover shedding, abrasion, pulled yarns, removable covers, upholstery wetting and drying.
4. `co-je-panamova-vazba-kosikova-tkanina-posun-niti-a-pranie`
   - Explain grouped warp and weft yarns as a basket-weave derivative of plain weave.
   - Cover lower dimensional stability, seam movement, snagging, washing and the Oxford/hopsack boundary.

- Cotton Incorporated/CottonWorks for plain weave, basket weave, Oxford weave, yarn construction and textile testing.
- ASTM and AATCC methods for thread count, abrasion, snagging, colourfastness and dimensional change.
- EU Regulation 1007/2011 and GINETEX for fibre labels and care symbols.
