## 1. Pigment-dyed clothing

Public title: Čo je pigmentovo farbené oblečenie: patina, oter farby a starostlivosť

Canonical boundary: the article owns pigment-dyed clothing, pigment-dyed garments and surface colour fixed with a binder. It must distinguish the pigment system from garment dyeing as a production stage, fibre-reactive dyeing, printing and ordinary colour transfer.

Reader decisions include recognising surface wear, testing dry and wet transfer, separating designed patina from binder failure, washing dark pieces without long wet contact and knowing when a sticky or cracking surface should be returned rather than treated more aggressively.

Primary evidence: CottonWorks pigment and dyeing guidance, CottonWorks printing guidance, AATCC colour and crocking test references, GINETEX care symbols and EU fibre-labelling rules.

## 2. Stonewashed and enzyme-washed denim

Public title: Čo je stonewashed a enzyme-washed denim: čo úpravy znamenajú a ako prať

Canonical boundary: the article owns industrial stonewashing, biostoning and enzyme washing of denim. It is not a guide to reproducing industrial abrasion in a household washer and it must distinguish cellulase finishing from the already published general biopolishing and ordinary home washing of jeans.

Reader decisions include identifying deliberate seam contrast, understanding pumice abrasion and cellulase action, checking weak high-wear areas, preventing colour transfer and backstaining, and deciding when tears or rapid fibre loss exceed normal patina.

Primary evidence: peer-reviewed PubMed/PMC cellulase-denim studies, the North Carolina State University Journal of Textile and Apparel Technology and Management, CottonWorks denim and dyeing definitions, AATCC methods and GINETEX symbols.

## 3. Peach finish or peached surface

Public title: Čo je peach finish alebo brúsený povrch: jemnosť, lesk a starostlivosť

Canonical boundary: the article owns peach finish, peached fabric, peach-skin fabric and fine sueding created by controlled surface abrasion. It must distinguish the finish from fibre content, ordinary flannel napping, moleskin, velvet pile and damage caused by later abrasion.

Reader decisions include reading fibre composition separately from the finish, comparing nap direction, avoiding local polishing, controlling friction and pilling, treating stains without creating a halo and assessing loose fibres without unsupported environmental claims.

Primary evidence: CottonWorks mechanical finishing, sueding and sanding guidance, the peer-reviewed 2023 domestic-laundering microfibre study, AATCC appearance methods, GINETEX symbols and EU fibre-labelling rules.

## 4. Plisse and permanently pleated textiles

Public title: Čo je plisé a krčená úprava: trvalé sklady, teplo a pranie

Canonical boundary: the article owns plisse/plise, permanent pleats, heat-set pleats and intentionally crinkled surfaces. It must distinguish heat-set polyester pleats, chemically or structurally retained folds and removable pressed creases, and must never recommend recreating unknown pleats with an iron.

Reader decisions include checking fibre and construction, washing without crushing the folds, drying in the intended direction, avoiding uncontrolled ironing, storing without flattening and escalating valuable, lined or mixed-material pieces.

Primary evidence: CottonWorks heatsetting guidance, a peer-reviewed pleat-retention study, the Victoria and Albert Museum polyester-pleat investigation, Smithsonian textile-storage guidance, GINETEX symbols and EU fibre-labelling rules.
