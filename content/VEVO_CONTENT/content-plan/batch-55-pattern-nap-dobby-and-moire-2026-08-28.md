## 1. Pepito, houndstooth and Glen check

Public title: Čo je pepito, kohútia stopa a Glen check: rozdiely vo vzore, farbe a starostlivosti

Canonical boundary: one comparison guide owns pepito, kohútia stopa, houndstooth, dogtooth, shepherd's check, Glen check, Glen plaid and Prince of Wales check. Do not split those names into later standalone definition articles.

Reader decisions:

- distinguish a small block check, pointed houndstooth and composite Glen check;
- determine whether the motif is yarn-dyed and woven, jacquard/knitted or printed;
- align garment pieces and recognise distortion, colour transfer and surface shine;
- select washing and ironing from fibre content and the complete garment, not from the pattern name.

Separation from existing content: Madras is a yarn-dyed coloured check with deliberate colour-transfer concerns; herringbone is a broken-twill structure; tweed is a fabric family; colourfastness is a general performance property. This article owns visual identification and care consequences of the three related tailoring patterns.

Primary evidence: Museum at FIT menswear fabric glossary; Technical University of Liberec traditional-pattern research item; GINETEX care symbols; AATCC textile testing standards; EU textile-fibre labelling rules.

Product boundary: recommend ordinary liquid detergent only for an explicitly washable, compatible garment. Exclude tailored wool suits, glued fronts, leather trim and professional-care labels.

## 2. Barchet and fustian

Public title: Čo je barchet a fustian: počesané tkaniny, žmolky a správne pranie

Canonical boundary: one terminology guide owns barchet, barchent, flannelette/flanelet and the historically broader fustian family. The article must explain that trade and historical usage varies rather than asserting that every fustian has one construction.

Reader decisions:

- identify smooth face versus brushed reverse and distinguish nap from loose lint;
- separate modern washable cotton or viscose barchet from wool flannel and dense moleskin;
- diagnose flattened nap, pills, shrinkage, colour loss and seam distortion;
- choose washing, drying and brushing without promising restoration of worn fibres.

Separation from existing content: flannel owns a broad soft fabric term and wool/cotton variants; moleskin owns dense brushed cotton and pressure shine; corduroy and rips own visible ribs. This article owns the barchet/fustian terminology and one-sided brushing construction.

Primary evidence: Technical University of Liberec textile construction paper; Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus fustian record; Metropolitan Museum fustian sample book; GINETEX and AATCC.

Product boundary: recommend ordinary liquid detergent only for an explicitly washable cotton, viscose or compatible blend. A wool, lined, historic or professionally cleanable object requires its own care route.

## 3. Dobby fabric

Public title: Čo je dobby tkanina: malé tkané vzory, zatrhávanie a pranie

Canonical boundary: one guide owns dobby, dobby weave, dobby cloth and dobby fabric. Do not create separate articles for these English labels.

Reader decisions:

- distinguish a small repeated woven figure from print, piqué relief and complex jacquard;
- understand harness-controlled warp groups without claiming that dobby names a fibre;
- inspect floats, snags, seam slippage, shrinkage and differential reflection;
- wash shirts, table textiles or towels according to fibre, dye, construction and complete-product label.

Separation from existing content: damask owns reversible patterned table and bedding textiles; piqué owns relief constructions; plain weave, Oxford and panama articles own simpler weave families. This article owns small loom-controlled repeated figures and their local float risks.

Primary evidence: North Carolina State University repository; Pratt Textile Research Lab; Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus; GINETEX; AATCC.

Product boundary: recommend ordinary liquid detergent only for a washable compatible dobby article. Exclude metallic yarns, coated hotel textiles, silk, glued trim and professional-care labels.

## 4. Moire

Public title: Čo je moaré: zvlnený lesklý efekt, voda, tlak a bezpečné čistenie

Canonical boundary: one guide owns moaré, moire, moire antique and watered silk. It must distinguish an intentional manufactured finish from accidental optical interference in photos/screens and from unwanted pressure shine.

Reader decisions:

- identify a deliberate watered surface created by pressure, heat, moisture, rollers, chemistry or printing;
- distinguish moiré from satin weave, taffeta/faille rib, watermark-like stains and iron shine;
- treat water, pressure and local spotting as possible finish-changing inputs;
- decide when a simple labelled washable item is suitable for home care and when formal silk or a structured object needs a specialist.

Separation from existing content: taffeta owns crisp plain weave and water maps; satin owns long floats and fibre-independent sheen; rips owns transverse ribs; formal-dress care owns the complete garment decision. This article owns the deliberate watered finish and its sensitivity to pressure distribution.

Primary evidence: Smithsonian Museum Conservation Institute moire abstract; Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus moire record; National Park Service textile-object conservation guidance; GINETEX and AATCC.

Product boundary: show a product card only as a conditional option for a simple explicitly washable cotton, regenerated-cellulose or compatible synthetic article. Do not imply that silk moire, formalwear, historic pieces or structured accessories can be washed at home.
